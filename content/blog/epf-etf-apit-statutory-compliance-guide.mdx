# The Employer’s Guide to EPF, ETF, and APIT Compliance in Sri Lanka

Navigating employment regulations and statutory payroll contributions in Sri Lanka requires precision, strict adherence to calendar deadlines, and a deep understanding of Department of Labour guidelines. Whether you are an established domestic corporate or an international firm setting up operations in Colombo, non-compliance carries heavy surcharges and legal liability.

This guide clarifies the statutory obligations surrounding the **Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF)**, the **Employees’ Trust Fund (ETF)**, and **Advance Personal Income Tax (APIT)**.

---

## 1. Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF)

Established under the EPF Act No. 15 of 1958, the Employees' Provident Fund is a mandatory retirement savings scheme covering all private-sector and non-pensionable public-sector employees.

### Minimum Contribution Rates
- **Employee Contribution:** Minimum of **8%** deducted from total earnings.
- **Employer Contribution:** Minimum of **12%** paid entirely by the employer.
- **Total Statutory Remittance:** **20%** of monthly total earnings.

*Note: Employers may voluntarily contribute a higher percentage (e.g., 15%), but cannot contribute less than the statutory 12%.*

### What Constitutes "Total Earnings"?
Under Section 47 of the EPF Act, "earnings" includes:
1. Basic wages or salary
2. Cost of Living Allowance (COLA) and special allowances
3. Payment in respect of holidays and leave
4. Cash value of food allowances (where applicable)

*Overtime pay and discretionary annual bonuses are typically excluded from the EPF earnings base.*

---

## 2. Employees’ Trust Fund (ETF)

Created under the ETF Act No. 46 of 1980, the ETF serves as an additional social security and life/disability insurance mechanism for the working population.

- **Contribution Rate:** Exactly **3%** of total monthly earnings.
- **Payor:** **100% employer-funded**. No deduction is made from the employee's salary.
- **Remittance Deadline:** Payable to the ETF Board on or before the last working day of the month following the salary month.

---

## 3. Advance Personal Income Tax (APIT)

APIT is the statutory withholding tax deducted at source by employers on remuneration paid to resident and non-resident employees under the Inland Revenue Act No. 24 of 2017 (as amended).

### Withholding Table & Progressive Slabs
Employers must deduct APIT based on the official Inland Revenue Department (IRD) withholding tables. Withholding tax kicks in once an employee's total monthly taxable employment income exceeds the statutory tax-free threshold (currently LKR 100,000 per month or LKR 1,200,000 per annum).

```
Taxable Monthly Income Band (LKR)       Marginal Tax Rate
-----------------------------------------------------------
First 100,000                              0% (Exempt)
Next 41,667 (up to 141,667)                6%
Next 41,667 (up to 183,333)               12%
Next 41,667 (up to 225,000)               18%
Next 41,667 (up to 266,667)               24%
Next 41,667 (up to 308,333)               30%
Balance (Above 308,333)                   36%
```

---

## 4. Penalty Framework for Late Remittances

The Department of Labour enforces stringent surcharges on delayed EPF payments:
- **1 to 10 days late:** 5% surcharge
- **11 days to 1 month late:** 15% surcharge
- **1 month to 3 months late:** 20% surcharge
- **3 months to 6 months late:** 25% surcharge
- **Over 6 months late:** 30% surcharge
